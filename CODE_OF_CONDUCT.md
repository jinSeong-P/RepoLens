# Code of conduct

## Our commitment

RepoLens contributors, maintainers, and users are committed to making project
participation a respectful, harassment-free experience for everyone,
regardless of age, body size, disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics,
gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socioeconomic
status, nationality, personal appearance, race, caste, color, religion, sexual
identity and orientation, or technology choices.

We aim to build a community that is open, welcoming, diverse, inclusive, and
healthy.

## Expected behavior

Examples of behavior that supports the community include:

- showing empathy, patience, and respect for different experiences;
- giving and receiving specific, constructive technical feedback;
- focusing discussion on the user problem, evidence, and project constraints;
- acknowledging mistakes, correcting them, and learning from the experience;
- respecting privacy and avoiding disclosure of credentials or personal data;
  and
- prioritizing the health of the project and community over individual status.

## Unacceptable behavior

Unacceptable behavior includes:

- sexualized language, imagery, or unwelcome attention;
- trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, threats, or personal attacks;
- harassment in public or private project interactions;
- publishing another person's private information without explicit permission;
- deliberately exposing secrets, vulnerabilities, or identifying information;
- sustained disruption of discussions or review; and
- conduct that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional
  setting.

Good-faith disagreement about code, design, scope, or evidence is not
harassment. Critique ideas and changes rather than people.

## Scope

This code applies in repository issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews,
security reports, and other spaces managed by RepoLens. It also applies when an
individual officially represents the project in a public space.

## Reporting and enforcement

For a non-sensitive moderation request, open an issue through the repository's
public issue forms and include only the minimum public context needed. Do not
repeat abusive content or personal information unnecessarily.

For a confidential report, a credible safety concern, or a report containing
personal information, use the repository's **Security** tab and choose
**Report a vulnerability**. State clearly that the submission is a code-of-
conduct report. Although GitHub labels this mechanism as vulnerability
reporting, it provides the project's available private reporting channel. If it
is unavailable, open a public issue that requests a private maintainer channel
without including confidential details.

Maintainers will review reports in good faith, respect the reporter's privacy as
far as practical, and avoid conflicts of interest. They may remove, edit, or
reject contributions and may temporarily or permanently restrict participation
for behavior they determine to be inappropriate, threatening, harmful, or
disruptive. Knowingly false or retaliatory reports may themselves violate this
code.

## Enforcement guidelines

Maintainers will consider context, severity, intent, impact, prior behavior, and
willingness to repair harm. Possible responses include:

1. **Correction** — a private or public explanation and request to stop or edit
   inappropriate behavior.
2. **Warning** — a formal warning with conditions for continued participation.
3. **Temporary restriction** — a time-limited restriction from interaction or
   representation.
4. **Permanent restriction** — permanent removal from project participation or
   representation.

The project may skip steps when necessary to protect participants or the
community.
